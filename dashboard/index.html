When an AI model is moved from the scanner it learned on to a new hospital's scanner, subtle hardware and protocol shifts degrade diagnostic performance.

1

Different Scanner Hardware

Siemens vs other manufacturers produce slightly different image intensities, magnetic field inhomogeneities, and tissue gradient contrasts.

2

Different Image Processing

Each hospital's software pipeline (e.g. FreeSurfer vs SPM segmentation) changes how brain volumes are measured and thresholded.

3

Different Patient Demographics

US cohort vs Spanish cohort have different age and sex distributions, introducing subtle biological covariates into cross-domain classification.

4 Numbers Per Patient. That's It.

Instead of uninterpretable deep black-box representations, we focused on biologically validated brain morphometry.

🧠

Brain Volume Ratio

Brain size ÷ Skull size. Alzheimer's shrinks the brain over time.

eTIV / ICV

Total Skull Size

Used to normalize brain measurements across people with different head sizes.

📅

Alzheimer's risk increases significantly with age.

Included to control for known differences in brain structure.

Simple features = reproducible science. No black box. Anyone can verify our results.

What We Found

Three primary conclusions summarizing the feasibility of cross-scanner medical AI translation.

01

Gap is Real

Every model dropped 13-16% AUROC when tested on a different country's scanner. Cross-scanner domain shift is a genuine clinical deployment problem.

02

ComBat Works

Statistical harmonization reduced the generalization gap from 0.14 to just 0.04. A 71% gap recovery using only 11 unlabeled target samples.

03

DANN Needs More Data

Deep learning adaptation failed here because only 11 Spanish calibration samples were available. Larger datasets would likely fix this.

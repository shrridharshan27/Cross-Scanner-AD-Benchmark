1. Executive Summary

This empirical benchmark investigates the clinical deployment barrier of cross-scanner domain shift in neuroimaging machine learning for Alzheimer's disease (AD) detection. While binary classifiers (AD vs. CN) achieve robust discrimination within single-scanner, homogeneous protocols, their predictive accuracy degrades sharply when deployed across independent clinical sites without local retraining.

We trained three baseline architectures (Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines with Platt scaling, and Multilayer Perceptrons) on the OASIS-1 cohort (US, Siemens Vision 1.5T, N=128), strictly controlled for patient age (≥ 60) and diagnostic confidence. Models were evaluated both internally (held-out test split) and externally on an independent Spanish MRI Cohort (Zenodo Record 3935636, N=54). We subsequently investigated whether statistical batch harmonization (ComBat) or deep domain-invariant feature learning (DANN) could eliminate the cross-scanner generalization gap using a minimal calibration set of only 11 unlabeled target scans.

2. Dataset Cohorts & Patient Population

To prevent confounding, rigorous inclusion criteria were established. OASIS-1 was filtered for individuals aged ≥ 60 with definitive diagnoses (CDR 0 for Cognitively Normal; CDR ≥ 1 for Alzheimer's Disease; CDR 0.5 MCI subjects were excluded to ensure a clean binary boundary). The Spanish cohort was similarly restricted to clinical AD (ea) and healthy controls (crl), excluding bipolar disorder (tb).

3. Volumetric Feature Unit Audit & Discovery (Task 0)

During initial testing, all baseline models produced near-random performance on the external Spanish cohort (~0.50 AUROC). An automated volumetric audit revealed a profound 1000× scale discrepancy between FreeSurfer and SPM analytical pipelines.

Forensic Diagnosis: The 1,000× FreeSurfer vs. SPM Scale Discrepancy FreeSurfer documentation labels intracranial volume (eTIV) as mm³, yet its export tables scale values into cm³/mL (~1,448). Conversely, SPM tissue volume summation accumulates physical voxel counts (1.5 × 1.5 × 1.5 = 3.375 mm³), resulting in raw cubic millimeters (~1,881,555 mm³). Standardizing an input of 1,881,555 against the OASIS-1 training mean (1,448) placed the feature at +13,635 standard deviations, driving logistic activations to 1.0 for all patients.

4. Leakage-Controlled Experimental Design

To guarantee research integrity and clinical reproducibility, two independent tracks were executed across 3 stratified random seeds (42, 123, 456):

  • Track A (Strict Zero Adaptation): Models were trained solely on the OASIS-1 training split (60%, N=76). Feature scalers (StandardScaler) and decision thresholds (maximizing Youden's J on validation split, 20%, N=26) were frozen and applied out-of-the-box to the held-out OASIS-1 test split (20%, N=26) and the entire external Spanish cohort (N=54).
  • Track B (Low-Data Harmonization): 11 unlabeled scans from the Spanish cohort (20% calibration split) were exposed for harmonization alongside the OASIS-1 training split. External evaluation was performed strictly on the remaining quarantined 43 Spanish subjects (N=43). Diagnostic labels were never seen during ComBat fitting or DANN adaptation.
Guaranteed Zero-Leakage Invariants
  1. StandardScaler fitted strictly on OASIS-1 train features; never exposed to test or target data.
  2. Decision thresholds frozen on validation split using Youden's J (J = Sens + Spec - 1).
  3. ComBat fitted strictly on source train + target calibration; evaluation subjects strictly quarantined.
  4. DANN domain discriminator trained purely on binary domain indicators; target disease labels never accessed.

8. Mathematical Formulations

ComBat Empirical Bayes Location/Scale Harmonization

ComBat standardizes feature distributions across scanning sites while conditioning on preserved biological covariates (Age and Sex):

y_{ijg} = α_g + X_{ij}β_g + γ_{ig} + δ_{ig}ε_{ijg}

where α_g is the baseline feature intensity, X_{ij} is the biological covariate design matrix, γ_{ig} and δ_{ig} represent additive location and multiplicative scale batch parameters for scanner i, and ε_{ijg} ∼ N(0, σ_g²). Harmonized features are calculated using empirical Bayes shrinkage estimators:

y_{ijg}^{ComBat} = (y_{ijg} - α̂_g - X_{ij}β̂_g - γ_{ig}*) / δ_{ig}* + α̂_g + X_{ij}β̂_g

Domain-Adversarial Neural Network (DANN)

DANN incorporates a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) to induce domain-invariant representations via a minimax objective:

L(θ_f, θ_y, θ_d) = (1/n_s) ∑ L_y(C(F(x_i^s)), y_i^s) - λ [ (1/n_s) ∑ L_d(D(F(x_i^s)), 0) + (1/n_t) ∑ L_d(D(F(x_j^t)), 1) ]

Dynamic adaptation schedule: λ_p = (2 / (1 + exp(-10 · p))) - 1, where p = epoch / max_epochs.
